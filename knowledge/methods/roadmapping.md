# Method: Roadmapping

A roadmap communicates **where the product is going and why** — it aligns stakeholders on direction and
priorities. It is a strategic communication artifact, not a delivery commitment or a dated feature factory.
The best roadmaps are organized around **outcomes and themes**, tied to strategy, and honest about uncertainty.

## What a roadmap is for
- Connect day-to-day work to vision and strategy (see `product-strategy.md`).
- Align cross-functional stakeholders on priorities and sequence.
- Set expectations about *what* and *roughly when*, while preserving flexibility to learn and adapt.

## Roadmap formats

**Now / Next / Later** (recommended default)
- **Now** — committed, in active development; highest confidence.
- **Next** — coming up; shaped but not committed in detail.
- **Later** — directional; problems/opportunities we intend to address, low time-precision.
- Confidence and detail decrease as you move right. Avoids false-precision dates while still communicating order.

**Theme-based**
- Organized around outcome-oriented themes (e.g., "Reduce onboarding friction") rather than features.
- Keeps the focus on problems to solve and value to create; teams retain latitude on solutions.

**Timeline / Gantt-style**
- Calendar-based with dates/milestones. Use when external commitments, dependencies, or hard deadlines
  genuinely require date precision (e.g., regulatory, contractual, marketing launch). Use sparingly — dates
  invite the roadmap to be read as a promise.

## Building a roadmap
1. Anchor on strategy and goals/OKRs — every item should ladder up to one.
2. Gather candidate initiatives from discovery, the backlog, stakeholders, and data.
3. **Prioritize** them (see `prioritization-frameworks.md`) against the current goals.
4. Group into **themes**; sequence by value, dependency, and capacity.
5. Assign confidence/horizon (Now/Next/Later) rather than spurious exact dates where possible.
6. Annotate assumptions and the outcomes each theme targets.

## Audience views
Tailor the roadmap to each audience (see `communication-standards.md`):
- **Executives** — themes, outcomes, strategic bets, big rocks; minimal feature detail.
- **Customers / sales** — directional value and rough timing; avoid hard commitments you can't keep.
- **Engineering / internal** — more granularity, dependencies, and near-term detail.

## Keeping it alive
- Treat the roadmap as a living artifact: review and re-prioritize on a regular cadence and when evidence
  changes. Communicate changes and the reasons.
- Distinguish **committed** (Now) from **exploratory** (Later) so changes downstream don't read as broken promises.
- Pair the roadmap with the goals it serves so viewers see *why*, not just *what*.

## Anti-patterns
- A dated feature list with no link to outcomes ("feature factory roadmap").
- Treating Later items as commitments.
- Over-precise dates on low-confidence work.
- A roadmap nobody revisits — it drifts from reality and loses trust.

## Quality checklist
- [ ] Every item ladders up to a goal/OKR and a customer/business outcome.
- [ ] Format matches the need (Now/Next/Later by default; dates only where truly required).
- [ ] Confidence/horizon is explicit; committed vs. exploratory is clear.
- [ ] Tailored views exist for the key audiences.
- [ ] It's reviewed on a cadence and changes are communicated with reasons.
